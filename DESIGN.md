# Stone Clear — working design brief

Motion: Expressive, selected by Kyle on 4 November 2026. CSS entrance orchestration, image reveals, active project selectors and restrained scroll detail. Reduced motion removes travel, smooth scrolling and animation delays.

Purpose: help homeowners and commercial property managers understand whether their existing stone can be restored; primary action is a free demonstration and quote via verified telephone/email. Service selector does not collect personal details or send anything.

Verified identity: Stone Clear, Carlow; blue fan-shaped logo recovered from stoneclear.ie. Original typeface is unverified; comparison with Arial Bold, Arial Black, Arimo and Archivo suggests Arial Black as the closest original style. Kyle requested a close-match primary with a secondary face. Current system: Archivo variable (headlines 750–800, interface 500–650; logo outlines 900) and Lato body (400/700), locally hosted through next/font. Archivo supplies the broad, heavy grotesque character; Lato’s lighter humanist shapes support longer copy. Readable 14–18px body and compact 13–14px navigation. Colours: Deep stone #172a38 for text, Chalk #f4f6f7 for surfaces, Clear blue #1258a4 for action, Ice #e5edf3 for alternate surfaces, White #ffffff, Slate #566773 for secondary text. White/blue controls; deep stone/chalk reading. Kyle requested a clean monochrome SVG: the original ten-blade fan has been redrawn with clean Bezier edges; Stone Clear is outlined in Archivo 900. No red tagline, reflection, bevel, gradient or embedded raster. All visible vector fills use Clear blue #1258a4. Standalone full-logo and symbol SVGs are in public/assets/.

Directions considered: dark stone atelier (too heavy for broad household cleaning); utility-led cleaning site (underplays workmanship); chosen architectural restoration journal. Reference: Fieldwork concept in sales-website-sample/references/images/concept-fieldwork.png. Adapt asymmetric image dominance, project indexing and fine dividers; use brighter blue identity, restoration-specific outcomes and actual material photography rather than architectural portfolio copy.

Hero job: inspect real workmanship. Large Carrara/Emperador floor photograph occupies the right and lower visual field, with a quiet vertical material annotation. Oversized left-aligned headline sits above/partly beside the image, with readable copy and blue action below. An offset compact before/after card bridges into the results section. No essential text overlaps busy stone. Mobile brings headline/action first, then a substantial crop of the real floor and its caption; secondary photo is removed from the narrow opening.

Page rhythm: hero and material strip; interactive restoration pair; editorial service index; domestic/commercial split; genuine assessment/process sequence; enquiry callout. Services groups the existing broad offer. Stone Restoration explains treatment and materials. Gallery lets visitors inspect authentic pairs and individual after images. About preserves established business facts. Contact uses an optional service selector followed by real contact links, no personal fields or submission simulation.

Signature updated at Kyle’s request: a native range control reveals the before image over the after image, with a visible divider and handle. It sweeps between 15% and 85% with a 4-second eased pass in each direction. Manual pointer/keyboard input pauses playback; an explicit Play/Pause control resumes/stops it. Autoplay waits for both images, stops offscreen or when the document is hidden, and respects both OS and site reduced motion. Original photographs differ in viewpoint, which is disclosed beside the comparison; they have not been warped or edited to imply alignment. No invented treatment detail, stock or generated outcomes.

All facts/assets are in notes/SOURCES.md. No customer ratings or testimonial section: Maps listing found, review text unavailable. No unverified client-logo wall, guarantees, response times or prices. Noindex remains enabled.

Client history: compact monochrome logo rail after the homepage material strip, with touch/trackpad/keyboard scrolling and labelled SVG arrow controls. No autoplay competes with the restoration comparison. Full responsive Clients wall uses real sourced marks, property captions and a clearly labelled former Westin name. Unsourced marks use plain text. All device sizes use SVG icons and text, with no emojis; explicit phone/tablet requirement.
